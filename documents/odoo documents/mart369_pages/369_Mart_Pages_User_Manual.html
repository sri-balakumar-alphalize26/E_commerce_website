The Shop Footer and Its Pages
User Manual  |  369ai

What this does

369 Mart Pages looks after the strip at the foot of every shop page — the footer — and the pages it links to: Terms of use, Privacy policy, FAQs, Contact us, About 369 Mart and the rest. You write each info page in the backend with a simple editor, choose which footer column it sits under, and see the real shop page beside your text.

The Footer settings hold the rest of the strip: the line under the logo, the app download buttons, the “We accept” payment chips and the categories in the Shop column. Everything here is public — it is what any shopper reads on the website — so write it as your shop's own word.

How the manual is arranged

Part 1 – Setting Up covers the footer strip and the categories it lists, done once. Part 2 – Everyday Use is writing and changing the info pages, which you will come back to whenever a policy or a phone number changes. Part 3 – Keeping It Secure & In Order covers who can change what, and what is public. Reference tables, quick tips and fixes follow.

Part 1 — Setting Up

Done once by a Manager or the Owner, from the 369 Mart menu.

1Where everything lives

Open 369 Mart from the main menu. This module adds two screens under Page building, plus one switch in the category list:

  • Page building → Info pages — every page the footer links to, one row each.
  • Page building → Footer — the footer strip: tagline, app links, Shop column, payment chips.
  • Advanced → Catalog (all views), list view — the In footer switch on each category (Owner only).

2Set up the footer strip

Open Page building → Footer. It is one form for the whole shop, in three blocks.

Brand

  • Line under the logo — a short tagline. Default: Computer parts and gear. In minutes, or in days.
  • Android app link — your Play Store address. Leave it empty and the Android button is hidden.
  • iPhone app link — your App Store address. Leave it empty and the iPhone button is hidden.

Shop column

  • Categories in the footer — how many categories the Shop column lists when you have not picked any yourself. Default: 5.

We accept — one payment method per line; each line becomes a chip at the foot of every page. The default list is UPI, Credit & debit cards, Net banking, Cash on delivery and 369 Wallet. Clear the box and the whole row is hidden.

Tip: List only the payment methods you really take. If you switch off cash on delivery elsewhere, remove Cash on delivery here too — this box does not update itself.

3Choose the categories in the Shop column

The Shop column lists product categories. There are two ways to fill it:

  • Automatic (nothing picked) — the first main categories, in your catalogue's order, up to Categories in the footer.
  • Your own pick — open Advanced → Catalog (all views), switch to the list view and turn on In footer for each category you want. Drag the rows to set their order. As soon as one category is ticked, only ticked ones are listed and the number above is ignored.

Either way, a category only appears if its In the app switch is on and it has an App address. When the shop has more main categories than the footer lists, an “All categories” link is added.

Watch out: The hint on the Footer form points to “369 Mart > Catalogue > Categories”. The In footer switch is actually in Advanced → Catalog (all views), in the list view, which only the Owner can open.
Part 2 — Everyday Use

Writing and updating the footer pages. Do this whenever a policy, phone number or delivery promise changes.

1The Info pages list

Open Page building → Info pages. Each row shows the Title, Footer column, Page type, Address and an Active switch. Switched-off pages stay in the list so you can turn them back on. Drag rows by the handle to set the order of links inside a column.

These 12 sample pages are there from the start:

Watch out: All of the sample wording is placeholder text, and some of it may not match how your shop actually works — for example, how refunds are paid, how fast you reply to a complaint, or which buttons the app has. Read and correct every page before going live. Terms of use and Privacy policy also open with a bold reminder line starting “Edit this page …”; shoppers see that line until you delete it.

2Write or change a page

Open a page, or press New. The form is split in two: what you edit on the left, the real shop page on the right.

  • Title — the page heading and the footer link's wording, e.g. Terms of use. Required.
  • Summary — one line under the title, e.g. How we look after your data. Optional.
  • Footer column — Help (default), Company, Policies, or Not in the footer.
  • Page type — A page of text (default), Questions and answers, Contact details or Delivery areas. See Step 3.
  • Address — the end of the page's web address on the shop, e.g. terms gives …/page/terms. Filled in from the title for a new page (lower case, words joined by hyphens); you can change it. Required, and no two pages may share one.
  • Active — off removes the page and its footer link from the shop.
  • Text — the body of the page. Select words to get the toolbar: bold, italic, links, bullets, numbers. You can paste from anywhere.

Press Save. The right-hand side then shows the page as shoppers see it. Before the first save it says “Save the page to see it here as shoppers do.”

Good to know: Colours, fonts and other styling pasted in from Word or a website are dropped on the shop; headings, bullets, bold and links are kept. The preview shows the saved page, so save again to see each change.
Important: Changing the Address of a live page breaks any link to the old address that you have shared, printed or put in a message. Change it only before going live.

3Page types

Every type shows your Text first. Three types add something under it:

  • Questions and answers — a Questions and answers table appears on the form. Add a row per Question with its Answer (the answer can have bold, links and bullets). Drag rows to reorder. The sample FAQs page has five.
  • Contact details — shows the shop's phone, email and address under the text. These come from Settings → Companies, not from this form. The sample Contact us and Grievance redressal pages use this type.
  • Delivery areas — lists the pincodes the shop delivers to, in pincode order, from the delivery settings, with each area's name, whether Quick and Express run there, and the delivery time.
Tip: The sample Grievance redressal page tells shoppers to write to the grievance officer “at the email below”, which is the shop's main email from Settings. If complaints go to a different person, put their name and email in the page's text.

4What the customer sees

At the foot of every shop page, the footer shows the line under the logo, the app buttons you filled in, the Shop column, then Help, Company and Policies, each listing its active pages in your order. A column with no pages is left out. The We accept chips sit along the bottom. Pages set to Not in the footer have no link but still open at their address, so you can link to them from elsewhere.

Changes can take about a minute to reach shoppers after you save.

Part 3 — Keeping It Secure & In Order

1Who can do what

RoleWhat they can do
OwnerEverything: create, change and delete info pages and questions; edit the Footer; tick In footer on categories.
ManagerCreate, change and delete info pages and questions; edit the Footer. Cannot reach the In footer switch.
Packer, Accountant, RiderCan open and read the pages and the Footer form, but cannot change anything.

The Manager's rights come with the Website designer right; any other user given Website designer can edit pages too. The Owner's come with Administrator (Settings).

2Keeping track of changes

Pages have no change history or notes log. Before a big rewrite of Terms of use or Privacy policy, copy the old text somewhere safe. To take a page down for a while, switch Active off instead of deleting it — the text stays ready to switch back on.

Good to know: Updating the module never puts the sample text back over your own wording.

3Privacy

Info pages and the footer can be read by anyone, signed in or not. Never put private details — a staff member's personal number, an internal note — in a page's text. Contact details pages publish the company phone, email and address from Settings, so check those are the ones you want shoppers to use.

Quick tips

  • Read every sample page before going live; the wording is placeholder text.
  • A footer column with no active pages is left out of the footer.
  • Save to refresh the preview on the right; it shows the saved page.
  • Switch a page off rather than deleting it if you may need it again.
  • Set the Address once and leave it; shared links depend on it.
  • Empty app link = no app button; empty “We accept” = no payment row.
  • Tick In footer on categories to choose the Shop column yourself.
  • Keep the company phone and email in Settings current; Contact pages show them.
